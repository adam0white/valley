## The casual phone player: report

**1. Verdict:** I'd put it down around 6 Ağustos, after about 50 taps, and I'd probably not come back. The child scene and the storm made me care for a minute, but I never understood the numbers, so I stopped trying. **Score: 4/10.**

**2. The first minute:** The title says "The Valley" and then gives me three paragraphs about an agha, a kahya, and "kile" I don't know. I skimmed it. Tapping "Take up the seal" worked. The first real screen is a map of little houses with dashed lines fanning out from mine, and a red "Call the hamlet to the agha's barley" button. That button hooked me enough to tap it.

**3. What I understood and what confused me:**
- I understood that people were now "putting the agha's barley off," and each one had a short quote. I could tap "Press Gülsüm" and she came. That was clear.
- I did not understand what "kile" is, what "due" means, or why the granary (30 kile) was still at 30 after I'd called everyone.
- The ledger says "The only numbers in the valley are his." It was a line I liked, but the numbers were a wall to me.
- Osman the reeve said "The agha lends, against next year, if you write to him." I tapped "Write to the agha for grain." The due went from 384 to 484 kile. I borrowed and owed more, and nothing on the screen told me that would happen.
- The feed is a long scrolling log. By day 19 it had about 50 lines, and I stopped reading each one.

**4. Best and worst moments (in-game dates):**
- Best: 20 Temmuz. "Word from the Çoban house: a child. Come and see the child, kahya, if you will." I went. Later the log said "Zehra of the Çoban house had a girl. They are calling her Gülsüm." This was the only moment a person felt like a person, not a number. The "You can't tell yet how Osman would answer" line on the Kavak card was also the first thing that made me want to walk down and talk to someone.
- Worst: 19 Temmuz onward, the refusals ("I remember what your office took.", "The last kahya said that too.") and the log growing. I could not tell what I was supposed to do about them. Also 20 Temmuz, the storm "flattened about 43 kile of the agha's standing barley." I only saw that by scrolling the log; nothing on the map showed it.

**5. Where I got bored, frustrated or quit:**
- The first time I closed the ledger I landed on the title page. The title said "Your ledger is open at 18 Temmuz", so I thought I'd lost the game. I had to tap "Open the ledger" to get back. A phone player would think they had quit.
- At steady speed, two minutes of waiting skipped nine days. I'm not sure I was ready for that. On the bus that is a lot to lose at once.
- I quit at 6 Ağustos because I had no clear next step, not because the game ended. On a bus with 15 minutes, I'd leave it open and forget it.

**6. The three changes that would most make me play again:**
1. Explain the numbers in one plain sentence, on the main screen. For example: "You need 484 kile by 15 Eylül. You have 54." Drop "kile" or define it once.
2. Make the consequence of a choice visible before I press it. "Write to the agha" should say "you will owe 100 more." Make the loan's cost clear before I tap.
3. Replace the log with a few short cards. Put the child, the storm and the refusals in front of me as single events I can tap, not 50 lines to scroll.

**7. Bugs and odd text:**
- "Bind this volume nowVolume II, 30 Temmuz of year one" runs the button label and the subtitle together with no space or line break. It looks broken. I did not tap it.
- On the map, the label "Kohak" overlaps "the agha's field" and a dashed line. The "Cloud over the ridge" caption sits on top of the map. Several house names collide with the dashed lines.
- After I tapped "Go" for the Çoban visit, the screen said "you are at the Çoban house," then 30 seconds later the card was still Kavak and the date had jumped to 21 Temmuz. I only learned the visit happened from the log later.
- Closing the ledger sent me to the title screen (see point 5).

**8. How far I got:** I stopped at 6 Ağustos, year one. The ledger at that point read: granary 54 kile, due 484 kile, rider comes 15 Eylül, owed for the loan 100, years short 0. Four screenshots and the diary are in the folder. The playtest driver is closed.

## The story-seeker: report

**1. Verdict.** The houses are written like people. The people are not. I wanted to know them and the game never let me talk to anyone. Score: 4/10.

**2. The first minute.** The opening does its work. "The agha lives in the city. You are his kahya… with no kin here." The line "The last kahya left in the night. Three pages are cut from his book" is a good hook. The map is a restrained hand-drawn sketch. Each house gets a one-line portrait:
- "Widow Saliha's house by the stream. The ox stall is empty."
- "Up the slope. The son is at the coffeehouse more than at the plough."
- "Two daughters, and the whole lane knows it."

I wanted to meet all of them. The next ten minutes did not deliver.

**3. What I understood, and what confused me.**
- **What I could do.** I could call the hamlet to a task, press a single house, send a few kile of grain, write to the agha for a loan and walk to a house. The agha's ledger is the only place with numbers, and it is a nice idea.
- **Walking to a house.** "Walk down and sit with them" gives no scene. The notebook says only "You walked down to the Kara house." The only effect is that my guess line is rewritten ("Their jars looked low.").
- **The wedding and the baby.** I went to the Kavak wedding on 26 Eylül and to the Çoban newborn on 20 Temmuz. Both gave one line of narration and no scene, no speech and no choice.
- **Pressing.** I did not know pressing had a cost until I did it. I pressed Zehra two days after she gave birth.
- **The Close button.** "Close the ledger" drops me on the title screen, which was disorienting.

**4. Best and worst moment.**
- **Best.** 22 Temmuz, Zehra, after I pressed her: "Zehra would not. 'The last kahya said that too.'" A day later: "I'm coming. Don't stand over me." That sounds like someone with a history. Close seconds: on 27 Temmuz Osman says "He lends. He never forgets that he lent." On 15 Eylül the rider "drank his coffee and said nothing, which is praise."
- **Worst.** 10 May (the call to the mill, 2 May, year two), where the notebook filled with about 70 stock refusals. Esma, a newlywed, says "I can't. My back's gone." Recep and Musa both say "After I sit at the coffeehouse." The same sentence rolls through different mouths.

**5. Where I got bored, frustrated or quit.**
- **The four-line rotation.** In July of year one every person is given the same four lines: "Our own barley first. Then the agha's." "Who feeds mine while I cut his?" "I'll come." "Fine. Before the rain." Osman and Emine both said "Fine. Before the rain." on the first day. The reeve and a child answer from one pool, so these are dispensers of text.
- **Contradictions.** The same person says yes and no on the same day (İsmail on 19 Temmuz, Osman on 19 Temmuz).
- **Fast speed.** At fast speed Gülizar of the Uzun house came to my door on 4 Ağustos: "Lend it to us. We will pay at the threshing." I had no way to answer. The only thing I could do was watch it go by.
- **No dread.** Everyone paid in full on 19 Ağustos, so "every house can say no" never bit.
- **Winter.** From 17 Ekim to 2 Mayıs the notebook read "The notebook is empty. Tap a house on the map." I quit there, around day 296 of the term, after about 75 commands.

**6. Three changes.**
1. Make visiting a scene. Give the household a few lines in their own voice, tied to what they are going through (the jars, the baby, the wedding, the dead ox). Let a visit change what they say later.
2. Give each person their own refusals and yeses. Remove the shared lines and have each person say no in terms of their own life. Stop "I'll come" and "I can't. My back's gone." appearing in several mouths. Zehra's "The last kahya said that too" is the standard to match.
3. Give me decisions in the story and fill the dead months. A door-knock like Gülizar's should be a prompt I can answer, and speed should slow down for it. Winter needs something to happen: a death, a quarrel, a debt, a letter.

**7. Bugs and wrong text.**
- "The crier has called for bring the share." (grammar)
- "Close the ledger" returns to the title screen, where I had to choose "Open the ledger".
- Two different people are called Elif (the Çoban newborn and the Kara wife).
- Zehra was told "Call them to the agha's barley" and "Press Zehra" two days after giving birth. The game offers it without comment.
- The notebook shows the same house's yes and no on one day.

**8. How far I got.**

## The EU/CK veteran: report

## Playtest report: The Valley (persona: grand-strategy veteran, about 2,000 hours of EU4 and CK3)

**1. Verdict.** I played the whole three-year term, and the elders' gathering at the end gave me a report card I want to beat. Getting there, though, I was mostly holding "fast" and reading excuses, and the one crisis that mattered happened while I wasn't watching. **6/10.**

**2. The first minute.** The setup hooked me straight away: a kahya with no kin, "Three pages are cut from his book", "Every house can say no." The house blurbs are excellent, for example "Up the slope. The son is at the coffeehouse more than at the plough." It cooled quickly. The agha's due was 384 kile against 30 in the granary, with rain in two days. I called the hamlet and the log filled with 74 or more lines of people coming to the agha's barley and putting it off. Sometimes one person did both at once (Recep, midday 19 Temmuz: "We'll be there." and "Not now. After I sit at the coffeehouse."), all under a status line saying "Many have come."

**3. What I understood and what confused me.**
- **What I understood:**
  - There are five kinds of lever: call the hamlet to work (free or paid), and on a house, ease its share, press it, send the reeve's men, send grain, or visit.
  - Visiting tells you how full a house's jars are. Easing a share comes with a fair warning: "half, this year; the lanes will see".
  - Memory is real. I sent the reeve's men to the Uzun house in year two. In year three Rıza answered a press with "I remember what your office took."
- **What confused me:**
  - **Where grain came from.** The granary jumped 90 → 335 → 431 with no log line saying why. I eventually guessed the agha's own field was being threshed.
  - **Sending grain.** "Send them grain" wasn't on house cards in year one, when Çoban was "scraping the jar", but it was there in year two.
  - **The paid call.** I tried "…for a sheaf in eight" in year three. The response was 60 came / 45 put off, worse than the free call the year before, and no line acknowledged the pay.
  - **No standing with anyone.** No card ever showed what a house thought of me until the gathering.
  - **Wasted visits.** Each visit was a single line, "You walked down to the X house."

**4. Best and worst moments.**
- **Best: 14 Ağustos, year three.** Rıza: "I remember what your office took." Hüseyin of Ceviz, pressed: "The last kahya said that too." Then the gathering on 16 Aralık of year three. Every line matched what I had actually done:
  - Kara, whose baby I visited: "I remember when you came to see the baby."
  - The three houses I never visited: "We hardly saw you. The crier did your asking."
  - Uzun, which I raided: "Your book will say what it says. Ours says something else."
- **Worst: 28 Mayıs to 14 Haziran, year three.**
  - Osman came to my door on 28 Mayıs ("Lend it to us…") and Mustafa on 3 Haziran ("Kahya, there is nothing in the jar. The children…"). The game never slowed down for either visit.
  - Then six people died in ten days. Saliha died old. Yusuf and Zehra of Çoban died the same afternoon. Osman the reeve, Hatice and Esma of Kavak all died at midday on 14 Haziran.
  - No cause was given, and I had about 140 kile sitting in the granary. Four children were left alone in the Çoban house.
  - If that was hunger, it's on me, but the game let me sleep through it. That is the opaque randomness I hate.

**5. Boredom and frustration.**
- **Empty winters.** From November to May there were no decisions. Hundreds of seconds of fast play passed under "The notebook is empty."
- **Repeated excuses.** Nearly every refusal at share time was "Not now. After I sit at the coffeehouse.", from widow's son to blacksmith.
- **The due was never at risk.** I met it all three years ("He drank his coffee and said nothing, which is praise.", the same line every year), so the agha side felt solved after year one.
- **Busywork.** The log spam turned reading into a chore.

**6. Three changes that would make me play again.**
1. **Slow down and alert me for door-knocks, hunger and illness, and give deaths a reason.** Then a crisis becomes a decision I make rather than a surprise I read about afterwards.
2. **Show a readable impression of me on each house card while I play, not only at the end.** Something like "they think you came only when you needed something". Visits should produce a line of talk too.
3. **Collapse the log into one daily summary per house.** Make each excuse fit the person, and make the paid call and the gifts visibly change answers. I want trade-offs I can see.

**7. Bugs and wrong-looking text.**
- "The crier has called for bring the share." (grammar)
- "Close the ledger" in the ledger panel dumped me to the title screen in year one.
- The ledger's "Shares this year" still showed last year's payments in Haziran of year two.
- Saliha's "The children…" — the Dere house is only Saliha and Musa.
- After the rider has been, the button still reads "the rider comes 15 Eylül", without saying it means next year.
- One "Go and sit with them" tap cleared three stacked, identical Kavak death cards.
- The Dere share can't be eased once the house is empty, and it still showed "0 of 7".
- Three babies in a row named Emin. Esra of Pınar had a boy named Emin twice: 19 Eylül of year two and 1 Haziran of year three.
- On the map, the "konak" and "the agha's field" labels overlap.
- In June of year two the event lines contradicted "Many have come": the visible lines were refusals.

**8. How far I got.** I reached the end of the term, 16 Aralık of year three (the elders' gathering, "The agha's letter renews your seal").
- The full due was paid every year: 384, then 440, then 440.
- Years short: 0.
- 56 kile were left after the last rider.
- In year two the due rose to 480 when the agha bought the upper hamlet, then fell by 40 when the mill opened.

### Exploits and spam
- **Year one pays itself.** I bullied for three days, vanished for a month, called shares once: due met with 43 spare, zero planning. The due is not a pressure in year one. Year two's shortfall was self-inflicted (loan + emptying the granary), so I cannot say whether a passive year two also pays.
- Pressing is free and always "works" ("came, slowly, because you pressed") even when the person later refuses; the refusal arrives as a separate line, so press looks like a lie the game tells.
- Reeve's men are instant and total: "took 21 kile from the Topal house's jars. The whole lane watched." No voice from the house, no cost shown that day.
- Spam guards held: shares call vanishes after one tap, "Send them grain" vanishes at 0 kile, Press becomes (hold) after the first use.

### Designer's critique
**Core loop:** call → come/put off → press → come grudgingly → rider takes the due; between, door visits ask for grain and you give or refuse. **Does it hold?** Half. The judging half holds: the ending correctly scored presence ("You never came down our lane", "The crier did your asking") and the people remember and resent (Fatma, Hatice: "I remember what your office took") and leave (Musa). The acting half does not: every verb the player owns — walk, sit, give, ease, send men — returns silence or a "your guess" line, while every memorable moment came to me unprompted. The game rewards presence and gives presence nothing to look at.
**One change:** make each player action in a house produce a spoken response from that house, in the moment, from a person with a name. That single channel would turn giving, visiting and pressing from ledger entries into relationships, and the ending's judgment would then feel like the sum of things I saw rather than a verdict delivered on a life I never got to watch.
