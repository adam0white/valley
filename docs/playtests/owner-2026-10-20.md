Confusions visible before implementation:

- The active screen never states the steward's overall purpose. The title's unfamiliar office names do not explain the balance between the landowner's due and households' lives.
- The first task appears in muted footer prose below several equally prominent notes. Rain, the letter, the granary key, the due, and the mystery of the last steward compete for attention.
- On day 12, the notebook starts with 25 July's old visits while the map says 30 July. There is no clear current event or invitation to act when the field is already cut.
- Day 12 repeats several hearth replies, in large handwriting. These occupy most of the phone sheet and much of the desktop sheet.
- House outlines and labels look like decorative map symbols. The residence, granary, mill, spring, fields, and houses share a drawing style although only households respond to taps.
- The hidden house buttons help screen readers but give sighted players no visible list or keyboard focus on the map itself. Small house symbols do not advertise touch targets.
- The first screen offers a harvest call and the ledger; a house offers grain but no visit yet. No explanation says that visiting becomes available after a household puts off a request.
- A call disappears while it remains open. The player sees neither how long the crier's request lasts nor when another call becomes possible.
- A visit disables another visit with only “You are out on the lanes.” It does not say that returning restores the action. Other actions need no walk, but this distinction is unstated.
- Giving can disappear when the granary runs low. The UI does not connect renewed giving to grain arriving at threshing or from shares.
- “kahya”, “agha”, “konak”, and “kile” carry essential meaning without English labels. Dates use Temmuz, Ağustos, and Eylül inside otherwise English instructions.
- The long footer mixes a numeric rider countdown, field status, immediate instruction, and a future threshing date in one quiet paragraph.
- Speed buttons and small history links lack a consistent hover/focus treatment. Several controls have targets shorter than a comfortable touch target.

## Delivery and regression evidence

The new charge states the steward's purpose in English. “Your charge” remains available during play. One current instruction leads above the map; the next seasonal event follows in quieter text. Day 12 now says the barley is cut and invites a visit while the sheaves wait for threshing. House controls are visible native buttons, with selected, hover, and keyboard focus states. Map house labels use link styling. Controls have touch targets at least 44 pixels tall and wide.

The notebook leads with a short current view. More notes open on request. Active calls have a separate “Read replies to the current call” control, with the person's actual reply and hold-to-press actions; these remain reachable after midnight and while history is open. House cards show only that house's active replies. Capacity explanations follow the crier, time spent walking, the crop's renewal, and grain entering the granary. No action counters were added.

Dates and essential office terms use a read-only English projection. Stored notes, calendar functions, simulation state, RNG, dependency versions, lab integration, and export code are unchanged. The private export restores and replays with the original fingerprint **e6948b88**, retaining all sixteen inputs. `test/owner-replay.test.ts` runs against the private file when `VALLEY_OWNER_EXPORT` is set; the file itself is not committed.

Regression coverage includes every season's next event, action renewal, pure presentation reads, three viewport layouts, keyboard-accessible house controls, history across midnight, visible active replies, touch targets, and visits becoming available after returning. Parallel reviewers found archived Press actions and stale task instructions; both were corrected.
