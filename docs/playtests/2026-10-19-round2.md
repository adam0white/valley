## The literary reader: report

**The Valley: playtest report (novelist persona)**

**1. Verdict.** The best sentences in The Valley are as good as anything in 80 Days. But around them the same few lines repeat, and the births and weddings have no scenes. The place feels lived in. The people stop feeling like people after the third visit. **5/10.**

**2. The first minute.** The cover hooked me: "The last kahya left in the night. Three pages are cut from his book." That is a missing predecessor and a debt, in two sentences. The ink-sketch map is handsome. Then I called the hamlet to the barley, and about 25 replies came back using roughly four lines. "Who feeds mine while I cut his?" is a terrific line once; by the eighth person it reads like a form letter. Only two replies had a voice of their own: "The agha's barley won't cut itself. More's the pity." (Esma) and "For the agha, then. He'll thank us personally, I'm sure." (Mustafa).

**3. What I understood, and what confused me.** I understood the houses, the reeve, the agha's due, and that every kindness is watched. Easing the Çoban share brought "Half. God bless you, kahya. Half we can carry." The next morning the coffeehouse said "The kahya has his favourites, then." That is the game's best mechanism.

What confused me:
- **I never found a way to give.** The Çoban house had a newborn and was "scraping the jar". I had grain, and the house card offered only "Walk down and sit with them". The only verbs I ever saw were call, press, ease, send the reeve's men, and write to the agha for a loan.
- **I could never seize.** "Send the reeve's men" only appears on unpaid shares, and every house paid.
- **The granary rose with no visible cause.** It went from 30 to 343 to 499 kile, and nothing in the log said why.

**4. Best and worst moments.**
- **Best: 12 Ağustos, year one.** Widow Saliha said "The last one smiled like that too, and then he took the ox." It ties the empty ox stall in her house description to the missing kahya. A close second came on 26 Temmuz: "The children have stopped asking. That's worse."
- **Worst: 20 Temmuz, year one.** The finest speech in the game was tagged to the wrong person: "Yusuf of the Çoban house, at the hearth: 'Kahya. My husband is in the field. Hold Nazlı, go on. Babies like strangers, God knows why. We boiled the last of the seed. God forgive us.'" Yusuf is a man. It happened again with Osman (the reeve) and with Hasan.

**5. Where I got bored and quit.** I lost interest on 26 Eylül and again on 18 Ekim. I accepted two wedding invitations. Each time I arrived, nobody spoke, and I walked back. The 20 Temmuz birth visit was the same. The greetings are a template: "You're the first of them to come to the door without wanting something. Or are you?" came word for word from Yusuf, Mustafa and Ömer. Yusuf said "We weren't expecting you" on my sixth visit. From the rider in Eylül to winter there was nothing to do. The winter log is pretty ("Snow on the upper lanes. The Tepe children were out on a tray until dark.") but it is filler. I quit on 7 Ocak of year two, with 251 days to the next rider and no decision left to make.

**6. Three changes that would bring me back.**
1. **Fix the speaker attribution and stop repeating lines.** A person should not say the same sentence twice, and two people should never share one. The hearth greeting should remember earlier visits.
2. **Give births, weddings and visits a scene with something to decide.** Someone should speak, there should be a choice (a gift, a blessing, a debt), and someone should remember it later.
3. **Add a visible "give" action and things to do between harvest and the rider.** Hunger in winter or spring, loans between houses, the predecessor's missing pages. Something to act on outside the harvest.

**On the quiet religious life: it feels natural, not tokenistic.** "At dusk Osman went down to the mosque, as he does." "Havva walked to the mosque through the snow for the night prayer, as most evenings." "God forgive us" and "thank God" sit inside speech the way they do in real Anatolian talk. It is never a mechanic. The one risk is the same repetition problem: these lines will turn into ritual filler if they recur as often as the goat lines do.

**7. Bugs and text that looked wrong.**
- **Gender bug:** "Yusuf / Osman / Hasan … 'My husband is in the field.'" The speaker tag shows the head of the household, not the woman speaking.
- **Uzun house:** "the Uzun house asks you to the wedding." There is no Uzun house on the map or in the house list, and the log never says who married.
- **Lower-case sentence starts:** "the Kavak house asks you…" and "the Uzun house asks you…".
- **Loan text contradicts the ledger:** the letter says "80 kile against next year's due", but the due this year jumped from 384 to 484.
- **Wrong status on rider day:** the bottom bar said "The rider is in the valley. The granary is nowhere near his due." while the granary held the due. That noon the rider "loaded 484 kile, the whole due."
- **Times and seasons:** "After the dew. I'll come." at midday. Roofs re-mudded "before the rains" in Kasım. Gardens turned at night in Kasım. Every winter entry is stamped "night".
- **False "as usual":** "Washing day … X brought the news, as usual" names a different person each time.
- **Repeated story:** "told the old story of the flood … They knew the ending and shouted it" came from two houses five days apart.
- **Doubled line:** Osman's "Some houses only open the door to a face." is printed twice in a row.
- **Implausible greeting:** the reeve's house and his brother's house both claim no kahya or nobody from the konak has visited in years, though the reeve stands at my elbow daily.
- **Map label:** "konak" is partly covered by a field glyph.
- **Driver:** `quit` threw "fetch failed: other side closed". The port stopped listening afterwards, so the driver did exit.

## What round 2 changed (iteration 2)

Each change below names the tester it answers. Commit and measures are in [design.md](../design.md), "Iteration 2".

- **Repeated lines** (reader, FM). Visit openers are scene-aware for a wedding, a birth or a death; a return visit within ten days opens with a line about coming again ("Again, kahya?
